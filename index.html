A1Make a mixtape

Hit REMIX on whatever's playing. Start with Lo-fi, House, Ambient or 8-bit, pick a speed, add some bass, swap the keys (Rhodes, guitar, pad or 8-bit), throw in rain or a purr. Every tap changes what you're hearing, and it never runs out: the beat keeps going and the tune keeps moving. Don't love the melody? NEW TUNE rolls another one with the same parts.

When it sounds right, save the tape. Catnip FM names it, puts it on a cassette and gives it a spot on the dial. Put your u/name in FROM and it says MIXED BY you wherever it plays. Send the link and somebody else's browser plays your mix live. Got someone else's tape? Hit REMIX and make it yours.

Made a good one? Drop the link in the comments wherever you found us, or send it to hello@catnipfm.com. The best ones go on the Catnip FM stream.

Side B · Liner notes

B1Meet Loafie

Loafie hangs out on the Catnip FM rooftop. She wanders around, loafs, grooms herself, watches the city and eventually falls asleep if nobody bothers her.

Pet her. Drop her a fish. Tap Z and she curls up for a nap. Touch the board and she's awake again. She has a cardboard box and strong opinions about when to use it. Don't leave anything on the ledge. The rest you'll figure out.

Tap the little cat key to change her coat: grey tabby, orange tabby, white, calico, Siamese or black. She keeps it, and so does the cat on your cassettes.

B2Focus: three stations

Hit FOCUS and step around the dial with ◀ ▶. None of these are recordings. Catnip FM composes them while they play, so another listen won't come out exactly the same.

Nine Lives
8-bit, between levels. Blow on the cartridge first.
Late Shift
Warm Rhodes and a lazy beat. Built for working late.
Rooftop
House drums, synth bass and a soft pad. The upbeat one.

B3Calm

Hit CALM for purr, rain, ocean, fireplace or brown noise. No songs, no talking, nothing demanding your attention.

B4Three looks

Tap the display key to flip through them.

808
Red, orange, yellow and cream glowing pegs. The default.
VFD
Cyan and orange, like an old stereo receiver.
Red LED
The clock radio on your nightstand in 1987.

Until you choose one yourself, the display can follow the music. After dark it settles down on its own.

B5The rooftop

Loafie spends her nights on a concrete ledge above the city. Windows come on and go off. A red light blinks on the tower. It's always night here.

When Rain or a rainy mixtape is playing, it rains across the whole rooftop. Sometimes it rains anyway. Friday and Saturday nights from 11, the skyline goes neon and the radio says LATE NIGHT VIDEO.

B6Leave it running

On a computer, Float keeps Loafie in a little window above whatever else you're doing. You can still pet her and feed her there.

Got an old tablet? Add Catnip FM to the home screen, leave it plugged in and you've got a little rooftop radio sitting on your desk.

Once it's loaded, it works offline.

B7Nerd stuff

Nothing you're hearing is an audio file. The drums, bass, keys, rain, waves, fire and purr are synthesized in your browser with the Web Audio API while you listen.

It's procedural, not AI-generated. Notes are kept inside the harmony underneath them, which is why you can stack different parts without immediately making a mess.

A mixtape link is just a handful of choices and a seed. The other person's browser makes the sound. There's no list of tapes anywhere, so nothing can run out: 1,200 recipes times about two billion tunes is roughly 2.6 trillion tapes that sound different, before you count the colours, the names and the spots on the dial.

No keyboard shortcuts on purpose. You're probably typing somewhere else and a stray key shouldn't stop the music.

The whole thing stays small because a rooftop radio with a cat on it shouldn't need 40 MB of JavaScript.
