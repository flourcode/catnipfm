Liner notes

Loafie, the rooftop, the stations, the displays and how any of this works.

A1Meet Loafie

Loafie hangs out on the Catnip FM rooftop. She wanders around, loafs, grooms herself, watches the city and eventually falls asleep if nobody bothers her.

Pet her. Drop her a fish. Tap Z and she curls up for a nap. Touch the board and she's awake again. She has a cardboard box and strong opinions about when to use it. The rest you'll figure out.

Tap the little cat key to change her coat: grey tabby, orange tabby, white, calico, Siamese or black. She keeps it, and so does the cat on your cassettes.

A2Focus: six stations

Hit FOCUS and step around the dial with ◀ ▶. None of these are recordings. Catnip FM composes them while they play, so another listen won't come out exactly the same.

Late Shift
Warm Rhodes and a lazy beat. Built for working late.
Rainy Window
A lo-fi beat, fingerpicked guitar and rain on the roof. Study mode.
Last Call
Trip-hop: a heavy, dusty break, deep bass and smoky Rhodes. Smells like 1996.
Rooftop
House drums, synth bass and a soft pad. The upbeat one.
Fire Escape
Synthwave: a gated snare, driving bass and neon pads. Sunglasses optional.
Nine Lives
8-bit, between levels. Blow on the cartridge first.

A3Calm

Hit CALM for purr, rain, ocean, fireplace or brown noise. No songs, no talking, nothing demanding your attention.

A4Three looks

Tap the display key to flip through them.

808
Red, orange, yellow and cream glowing pegs. The default.
VFD
Cyan and orange, like an old stereo receiver.
Red LED
The clock radio on your nightstand in 1987.

Until you choose one yourself, the display can follow the music. After dark it settles down on its own.

A5The rooftop

Loafie spends her nights on a concrete ledge above the city. Windows come on and go off. A red light blinks on the tower.

When Rainy Window, Rain or a rainy mixtape is playing, it rains across the whole rooftop. Sometimes it rains anyway.

A6Leave it running

On a computer, Float keeps Loafie in a little window above whatever else you're doing. You can still pet her and feed her there.

Got an old tablet? Add Catnip FM to the home screen, leave it plugged in and you've got a little rooftop radio sitting on your desk.

Once it's loaded, it works offline.

A7Nerd stuff

Nothing you're hearing is an audio file. The drums, bass, keys, rain, waves, fire and purr are synthesized in your browser with the Web Audio API while you listen.

It's procedural, not AI-generated. Notes are kept inside the harmony underneath them, which is why you can stack different parts without immediately making a mess.

A mixtape link is just a handful of choices and a seed. The other person's browser makes the sound.

No keyboard shortcuts on purpose. You're probably typing somewhere else and a stray key shouldn't stop the music.

The whole thing stays small because a rooftop radio with a cat on it shouldn't need 40 MB of JavaScript.
